# Key terms and legal basics (India)

**SARFAESI Act, 2002** — The Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of
Security Interest Act. It lets banks take possession of a property given as security for a loan and
sell it (usually by public e-auction) without going to court, when the borrower's loan becomes an NPA
(non-performing asset). Sales follow the Security Interest (Enforcement) Rules, 2002 — for example a
public sale notice is published (normally at least 30 days before the sale for immovable property).

**DRT / Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act** — Some auctions are conducted by the Debt Recovery Tribunal
recovery officer instead of the bank directly. The process is similar, the seller is the recovery officer.

**IBC / Liquidation sales** — Under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code a liquidator may auction company assets.

**Reserve price** — The minimum price set by the bank. Bids below the reserve price are not accepted.

**EMD (Earnest Money Deposit)** — A refundable deposit, commonly around 10% of the reserve price, paid
before the auction to be eligible to bid. Refunded if you lose; adjusted in the price if you win.

**Bid increment** — The minimum amount by which each new bid must exceed the previous highest bid.

**Physical possession** — The bank has actually taken over the property (keys / sealed). The buyer can
usually get possession soon after paying. Generally lower risk.

**Symbolic possession** — The bank has legal possession on paper but the property may still be occupied
(for example by the borrower). Getting actual possession can take extra time and may need a District
Magistrate order. Generally higher risk and often priced lower.

**"As is where is, as is what is, whatever there is" basis** — The property is sold in its current
condition, with any defects and (unless the notice says otherwise) any pending dues. The buyer must do
their own checks.

**Freehold vs Leasehold** — Freehold: you own the land/property outright. Leasehold: ownership for a lease
period from a lessor (e.g. development authority); transfer may need lessor permission and charges.

**Encumbrances / dues** — Unpaid property tax, society maintenance, electricity or water bills. The sale
notice may say they are payable by the buyer — always check.

**Sale certificate** — The document the bank issues to the successful bidder after full payment; it must
be registered with the sub-registrar.

**Borrower's right of redemption** — Under SARFAESI the borrower can stop the sale by paying all dues
before the sale notice is published (as per the amended law). After that, the sale can proceed.

Disclaimer: this is general information, not legal advice. Laws and bank terms change; consult a lawyer
and read the official sale notice before bidding.
